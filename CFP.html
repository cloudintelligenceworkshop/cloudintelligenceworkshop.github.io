Overview

Cloud engineers face two related challenges: using AI to operate large cloud services, and keeping the services that run AI reliable and efficient. These challenges meet when, for example, an on-call engineer relies on a large language model (LLM) to interpret telemetry during an outage.

For cloud operations, an assistant can help engineers trace a failure, coordinate an incident response, or suggest a recovery action. The hard question is when to act on that suggestion: what evidence supports the diagnosis, how can the team check that a fix will not spread the failure, and when should a person intervene? Outside of incident response, teams can use workload data to decide when to scale services and which workloads can share machines, reducing both cost and carbon footprint.

For AI-powered services, monitoring infrastructure is only part of the task. Engineers also need to evaluate how models and agents behave in production and plan capacity for training, inference, and multi-step agent workflows. A scheduling decision that lowers cost may also increase response time or make a service less reliable.

The workshop invites researchers and practitioners to bring early results, project demonstrations, and lessons from operating real systems. Building on editions at AAAI, ICSE, MLSys, and ASPLOS since 2020, we aim to identify problems that teams in academia and industry can investigate together.

  • Predictive capacity management

  • AI/cloud observability and anomaly detection
  • Testing and evaluating AI services and agents
  • System configuration management
  • Hardware/software failure prediction
  • LLM-assisted diagnosis and root-cause analysis
  • Incident management
  • Training, inference, and agent efficiency
  • LLM assistants and agents for cloud operations
  • Safe remediation with human oversight
  • Data center management
  • Customer support
  • Security, privacy, and responsible AI

Submission Instructions

Submit original work that has not been published and is not under review elsewhere. Use PDF and the ACM two-column conference format (LaTeX: acmart with sigconf). Page limits include all content and references.

  • Technical papers (up to 6 pages): New research results, research visions, or position papers.
  • Extended abstracts (up to 5 pages): Preliminary results, work in progress, or positions intended to start a discussion.
  • Project showcases (up to 2 pages): Innovative cloud intelligence projects. Open-source, open-data, and social-good projects are especially welcome. A recorded demonstration and project URL are encouraged.

Submissions will be managed through Microsoft CMT. The workshop-specific submission link will be posted here when available.

Review and Publication

The program committee will peer-review all categories for novelty, technical quality, potential impact, interest, clarity, relevance, and reproducibility. The program co-chairs will select contributions for oral presentation based on the reviews and committee discussion.

Accepted contributions will appear only on this workshop website, not in the ACM Digital Library. No ACM article-processing charges apply to technical papers, extended abstracts, or project showcases.

Under SIGSOFT policy, organizers and their directly supervised students, postdocs, and employees may not submit. Conflicted committee members, including those from the same organization as an author, will not review or decide on a submission. An alternate chair will handle submissions for which a program chair has a conflict.

FSE asks workshop calls to include the following notice: “As a published ACM author, you and your co-authors are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM’s new Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects”.
